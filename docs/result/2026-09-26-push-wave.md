## Point-by-point assessment

**(1) Resolution plateau 768 ≈ 896 ≈ 1024 vs. seed noise.**
Only `img896_dp01_seed86` and `img1024_dp01_seed86` share a seed (86) and
recipe, giving one clean same-seed comparison: 896→1024 gains +0.0073 SWA
(0.4546→0.4619). That is a real, if modest, continued resolution benefit.
The 768px runs used *different* seeds (1024, 42), not 86, so the
768-vs-896-vs-1024 comparison as a whole is seed-confounded. For context,
seed-only variance at fixed resolution/recipe has run 0.0047-0.0065 in prior
sweeps (`seedsweep` 512px: 0.4369-0.4434, range 0.0065; `res_sweep` 640px:
0.4447 vs 0.4494, range 0.0047), and seed 86 specifically has landed in the
lower half of its seed cohort in every prior same-resolution comparison
(seedsweep 512: seed86 0.4385, 5th of 6; res_sweep 640: seed86 0.4447 <
seed1024 0.4494). Given that, `img896_dp01_seed86`'s comparatively weak SWA
(0.4546, actually below the 768px no-dp baseline) is plausibly partly a
seed-86 penalty rather than an intrinsic 896px problem, and the 768-seed42
result (0.4600) landing so close to the 1024-seed86 result (0.4619) is
consistent with a shallow-but-real resolution slope that is currently the
same order of magnitude as seed noise. **Net read: there is a genuine,
non-zero resolution gain 768→1024 (best isolated at +0.0073 same-seed
896→1024), but this wave cannot cleanly separate "1024 is better" from
"seed 86 plus 1024 happened to land well" because 896 and 1024 share a seed
that 768 does not.** A same-seed 768 run at seed 86 (or 896/1024 runs at
seeds 1024/42) would resolve this.

**(2) Drop-path 0.1 effect at 768 vs. `res_sweep img768`.**
There is no seed-86 768px run in this wave to compare directly against
`res_sweep/img768_seed86` (no drop-path) — the push wave's 768px slot only
covers seeds 1024 and 42. The one clean same-seed drop-path A/B in the whole
project is at 512px (`sweep2/fix_only/seed_86` 0.4349 vs `sweep2/dp01/seed_86`
0.4399, +0.0050 best-epoch), matching the submit script's own comment. At
768px+ the indirect evidence is suggestive but not conclusive: every dp0.1
run in this wave (768/896/1024, 4 of them) peaks at **epoch 3** with a mild
post-peak decline, whereas the no-dp `res_sweep/img768_seed86` baseline peaks
at **epoch 2** and declines more sharply thereafter (mAP 0.4560→0.4533→
0.4456→0.4270 by epoch 5). That "later, flatter peak" pattern is consistent
with drop-path's expected regularization effect, but `res_sweep/img640_seed86`
(also no drop-path) *also* peaks at epoch 3, so epoch-of-peak alone is not a
reliable drop-path signature — resolution and seed both shift it too. **Read
this as suggestive, not proven: no seed-matched 768px dp0.1-vs-no-dp0.1 pair
exists to isolate the effect at this resolution.**

**(3) SWA gain.**
All three 4-epoch scratch runs at 768px seed42 (+0.0015), 768px seed1024
(+0.0018), and 1024px (+0.0017) show SWA beating the best single epoch by a
consistent +0.0015-0.0018. `img896_dp01_seed86` is flat (+0.00001,
0.45460→0.45461). `cont1024_from768_s86` is the outlier: SWA (0.4393) is
*worse* than its own best epoch (0.4422, epoch 1) by -0.0029, because
`swa-last-k 3` with only 2 completed epochs averages epoch 1 (peak) with
epoch 2 (already regressed), pulling the average down rather than up. This
is larger than the SWA gain measured for the `res_sweep/img768_seed86`
baseline (+0.0006, 0.4560→0.4566) — plausibly because that baseline's peak
sits at epoch 2 of 5, so its last-3 SWA window (epochs 2,3,4) averages the
peak with two already-declining epochs, whereas the push wave's peak at
epoch 3 of 4 means the last-3 window (epochs 2,3,4) brackets the peak more
symmetrically (rising, peak, mildly-declining), capturing more of the good
weight-space region.

**(4) Overfitting after epoch 3.**
Confirmed in all four 4-epoch scratch runs: `train_loss` decreases
monotonically through epoch 4 in every case (e.g. img1024: 0.0428→0.0396→
0.0372→0.0343), while `mAP` peaks at epoch 3 and drops at epoch 4 in all
four (img1024: 0.4602→0.4561; img896: 0.4546→0.4476; img768/1024:
0.4564→0.4539; img768/42: 0.4585→0.4554). Notably, `val_loss` does *not*
always show the same clear signal — img1024's val_loss only ticks up
slightly (0.03858→0.03916) even as mAP drops by 0.0041, which is a reminder
that `val_loss` (BCE/ASL-style) and `mAP` (ranking metric) can diverge; the
run configs correctly use `--monitor mAP` rather than val_loss, which is
what actually caught this. None of the four hit early stopping
(`patience 2`) — they were capped by `--epochs 4` per the submit script's
explicit design ("stop at epoch 4"), so it is unknown whether mAP would have
continued to fall, plateaued, or bounced back at a hypothetical epoch 5;
prior sweeps with longer horizons (`res_sweep/img768_seed86`, 5 epochs) show
continued monotonic decline through epoch 5 (down to 0.4270), so a further
drop at epoch 5+ here would not be surprising but is not observed directly.

**(5) Why `cont1024_from768_s86` underperformed.**
It underperforms every other comparison point available: its own source
checkpoint (`res_sweep/img768_seed86`, best 0.4560 / SWA 0.4566) *and* the
scratch-at-1024 alternative (`img1024_dp01_seed86`, best 0.4602 / SWA
0.4619) *and* even the scratch-at-896 alternative (0.4546 best). Its best
result (0.4422, epoch 1) is already below the 768px source checkpoint it
started from, and its second (final) epoch drops further to 0.4338 with
val_loss rising immediately (0.0401→0.0416) — this run overfits within a
single epoch. Two design choices plausibly compound this: (a) it drops the
`--drop-path-rate 0.1` used by every other push-wave run (per the script's
comment, "matches the source weights" — the 768px source was trained
without drop-path), so it gets none of the regularization the other 1024px
run benefits from; (b) it uses a very short horizon (`--epochs 2
--sched-epochs 2`, LR fully decaying by epoch 2) starting from an
already-converged, already-somewhat-overfit 768px checkpoint rather than
Stage-1 raw weights, so there is very little "room" left in the schedule for
the model to productively adapt to the new resolution before the LR is
gone. Net effect: a short, no-drop-path, low-LR continuation from a
768px optimum does not transfer to 1024px as well as training from
Stage-1 raw weights directly at 1024px with drop-path and a full 4-epoch
budget — the cheaper "FixRes-style" continuation strategy this run was
testing did not pay off here.

**(6) Wall-clock cost per epoch by resolution.**
Steady-state per-epoch wall time (from consecutive `Epoch NN/N` log
timestamps, excluding the first epoch which includes job/data-loader
startup overhead):

| Resolution | Run(s) | GPU | Steady-state epoch time | SWA compute overhead | Total job elapsed (sacct) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 768 | seed1024 / seed42 | RTX 4500 Ada 24GB (petra) | ~2h24m / ~2h24m | ~24-25 min | 10:21:27 |
| 896 | seed86 | RTX A6000 48GB (juggernaut) | ~3h05m | ~37 min | 13:22:54 |
| 1024 | seed86 (scratch) | RTX A6000 48GB (juggernaut) | ~3h41m | ~38 min | 15:43:26 |
| 1024 | cont1024 (2 epochs) | RTX A6000 48GB (juggernaut) | ~3h40m (epoch 2) | ~39 min | 08:21:32 |

The 1024px scratch and continuation runs agree closely on per-epoch cost
(~3h40-41m) despite different init checkpoints and LR — a same-hardware,
same-resolution sanity check that this figure is a real resolution cost, not
noise. The 896→1024 step (same GPU, A6000) costs +19% more wall time per
epoch (3h05m→3h41m) for a same-seed +0.0073 SWA mAP gain. **The 768 number
is not directly comparable to 896/1024 on wall-clock terms — it ran on a
different, smaller GPU (RTX 4500 Ada 24GB vs RTX A6000 48GB) on a different
node, so the apparent ~1.3-1.5x speed advantage of 768px conflates resolution
and hardware.** Total elapsed time (including SWA and job overhead) scales
from ~10.4h (768px) to ~13.4h (896px) to ~15.7h (1024px, scratch) for the
four-epoch scratch recipe.

## Caveats / known-bug flags

- All five runs post-date the class-weight-order fix (`--class-weight-order
  shard`) and the metric-embed fix (`--metric-embed query` present in every
  `args:` line) — no pre-fix concerns for this wave.
- No per-class / head-medium-tail eval exists for any of the five
  checkpoints — the paper-revision "tail-analysis gap" confound applies
  here too; this wave cannot speak to tail-class behavior at all.
- The 384-vs-512 (and by extension 768-vs-896-vs-1024) resolution confound
  noted in `paper-revision-confounds` is directly relevant to point (1)
  above — treat the "resolution plateau" framing as provisional until a
  seed-controlled resolution grid exists.
- Wall-clock comparisons across 768 vs {896, 1024} are confounded by GPU
  hardware (RTX 4500 Ada 24GB vs RTX A6000 48GB on different nodes) — see
  point (6).
- 0.4619 SWA is internal validation only; no leaderboard/test submission
  exists for any push-wave checkpoint. Do not conflate with the MoE's scored
  0.4599 test result.

## Notable

- **New campaign best**: `img1024_dp01_seed86` SWA mAP **0.4619** (best
  single-epoch 0.4602 @ epoch 3), surpassing the prior recorded best
  (`res_sweep/img768_seed86` SWA 0.4566) by +0.0053. This is the first
  single-model checkpoint in the campaign log to approach the previously
  best-known *ensemble* number (wave-0 greedy-3 + flip-TTA, 0.4641).
- Counter-evidence to a "monotonic resolution improves everything" reading:
  `img896_dp01_seed86` (0.4546 SWA) sits *below* the older 768px no-drop-path
  baseline (0.4566 SWA) despite higher resolution and drop-path — resolution
  is not a free lunch at every step, and this wave cannot fully separate the
  896px result from a seed-86-specific penalty (see point 1).
- `cont1024_from768_s86` is a clear negative result for the
  continuation/FixRes-style strategy as configured (short horizon, no
  drop-path, from an already-optimum 768px checkpoint) — worth flagging to
  the orchestrator since the submit script's design rationale (comment block
  in `submit_stage2_v3_push.sh`) proposed this as a cheaper alternative path
  to 1024px, and it did not work.
